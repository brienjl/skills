# A small working map

Use this as a starting shape, not a required form. Keep existing equivalent artifacts. No separate profile, risk register, roadmap document, or decision log is needed by default.

```markdown
# [Idea name]

## Ambition and value
[Outcome we want, first customer and situation, current alternative.]
[Their key job/pain/gain, and how our offer could improve it.]
[Important constraints or exclusions.]

## Biggest unknowns
- Desirability: [Most consequential uncertainty, or relevant evidence.]
- Feasibility: [Same.]
- Viability: [Same.]

## Route
- Now: [Small outcome or learning step; link to actionable ticket.]
- Next, if [evidence]: [Next small value increment.]
- Later: [Broader direction and questions still too vague to ticket.]

## What we've learned
- [Date, decision gist and implication; link to evidence ticket.]

## Pick up here
[Next action and why; anything waiting on input, access, or results.]
```

Start with a few bullets under each heading. Omit empty sections; use plain names and links instead of introducing an ID system for every claim. Preserve critical uncertainty without cataloguing everything that might go wrong.

## Example

Illustrative hypotheses, not customer evidence:

- **Ambition/value:** Help independent repair shops spend less staff time confirming appointments. We believe repeated calls are a meaningful interruption; a managed reminder service might reduce them.
- **Risks:** Do shops care enough to switch? Can their schedule/contact data support reliable reminders? Would a fee or budget cover messaging and actual support labor?
- **Now:** Observe the current confirmation process with a few recruited shops. Test whether the interruption matters and establish a baseline.
- **Next, if the problem is supported:** Try a one-week concierge reminder service for one shop. It delivers a small real benefit without first automating everything.
- **Decision to define before the pilot:** What improvement in staff time, error rate, and continuation commitment would justify the cost of automation? Proposed thresholds need a basis, not a claim of universal validity.
- **Later:** Automate the workflow the pilot supports; consider other segments after that.

If staff time improves but the service costs more than anyone will fund, preserve the positive outcome evidence and revisit delivery or economics before building more.

## When more detail earns its place

Split a section into a linked document only when it becomes hard to scan or must be maintained independently: for example, competing customer segments, substantial research, or a protocol another person must run. Keep a short summary and link in the map. Use stable claim identifiers only when cross-references become difficult without them. Reuse the team's existing material instead of adding parallel documentation.
